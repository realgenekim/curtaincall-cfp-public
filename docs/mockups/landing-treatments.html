Everything we hate about CFP tools

  • Reviewers can't actually talk to each other — opinions go to die in silos
  • Committee members forced to log in to yet another tool just to leave a comment
  • Form-filling, forever. It turns the best part of running a conference into a chore
  • Deciding and notifying smeared into one terrifying button
  • Your program, trapped — export means copy-paste
  • Exhibit A of our desperation: Trello boards, Zapier glue, one Google Sheet running the schedule for ten straight years, and an entire front-end we built by scraping our own CFP tool

What we're proud of in this one

  • Review is a conversation — every score and comment on one shared page
  • Committee members click one link. Speakers never make an account at all
  • Zero to open CFP in ten minutes — the acceptance test is a stopwatch
  • Decide quietly, then tell everyone deliberately — and it remembers who's been told
  • sessions.json, speakers.json, calendar.ics, a real API — your site drinks directly
  • Append-only ledger: nothing is ever deleted, everything can be rewound

Five incumbents implicated · 20,000 committee messages entered into evidence · beloved BusyConf mourned

For fifteen years, the conference desk of a certain Portland publishing house ran its calls for papers on five of the worst tools on the planet — and, per sworn committee testimony, was held hostage by every one of them. Workarounds date to 2014: Trello boards, Zapier relays, the notorious ten-year "Schedule Blocking" spreadsheet, and finally an entire replacement front-end constructed by scraping the incumbent's own pages.

Investigators reviewing the twenty thousand Slack messages describe a committee "referring to speakers by Google Sheets row number" (the Cvent year), reviewing by thumbs-tally (the eventPower year), and observing one vendor patch its application live during the weekly decision call. Only BusyConf, adopted 2018, drew praise — "the beloved era" — before dying of Heroku repricing in 2023, still mourned.

"Review is a conversation among trusted peers — not an evaluation plan."

The new tool, built in the open after S. Wang ("swyx") publicly dared readers to kill his software bill, opens a call for papers in under ten minutes, keeps every score and comment on one shared page, separates deciding from telling, and emits the exact open-data files a conference website drinks. Its ledger, engineers note, cannot delete anything — every change is a recorded fact. The proprietor has pledged the contest's $10,000 prize to STEM charity.

Treatment 03 · The Postmortem — the story as an incident report (engineers trust postmortems)
INCIDENT-2011-∞ · FILED 2026-08-09 · STATUS: RESOLVED

Fifteen years of CFP tooling failures, root-caused and fixed.

SEV-1 · DURATION: 2011 → 2026 · CUSTOMERS AFFECTED: every conference we ran
Impact
Five tools adopted, five abandoned. Committee reduced to referring to speakers by spreadsheet row. Review conducted by thumbs-tally. One vendor observed patching production during the decision call.
Workarounds
Trello (2016), Zapier, the ten-year "Schedule Blocking" Google Sheet, and a complete replacement front-end built by scraping our own CFP tool (2021–2025).
Evidence
20,000 committee Slack messages, 2014–2026, reviewed in full.
Trigger
2026-08-09: swyx dares the internet to kill his SaaS bill. We accept.
Resolution
Curtain Call. Open a CFP in ten minutes · review on one shared page · decide and tell as separate deliberate acts · append-only store (nothing deletable, ever) · sessions.json / speakers.json / .ics / API with stable IDs. Verified in production.
ROOT CAUSE

CFP tools are built for tool vendors' demos, not for the weekly call where five trusted people argue a conference into existence. Fix: build for the call. Two sorts are the only work queues; every opinion visible; the schedule tolerates half-decisions.
