## Price the friendship channel correctly

From swyx’s perspective, he is simultaneously a friend, a judge or organizer, and the steward of a fragile community experiment. The best friend behavior is to make the experiment better while making the friendship administratively invisible.

### What strengthens the friendship

**1. Create a public good that validates the premise of the contest.**

A serious research artifact says: “Your prompt caused worthwhile work, and the community benefited.” That is far more meaningful to an organizer than private flattery. It also embodies swyx’s stated Learn in Public values.

**2. Protect him from favoritism optics.**

Publish contest-relevant material publicly. Ask contest-relevant questions in shared channels. Do not request private coaching, early reactions, or interpretive guidance. A friend should understand the bind better than an ordinary entrant does.

**3. Submit something polished and operationally boring.**

A clean repository, obvious golden path, successful export, and two uncoached rehearsals reduce organizer risk. Friends who run events remember the people who created energy without creating cleanup.

**4. Help other entrants without turning generosity into theater.**

Answer questions. Let others reuse the diagrams. Congratulate good work. Do not keep announcing how generous Gene is being.

**5. Accept the result cleanly.**

No adjudication argument, wounded subtweet, post hoc scoring analysis, or “the judges missed…” narrative. The documentary must remain a study of the work—not an appeal of the result.

**6. Send one genuinely personal note after judging.**

Not during deliberation. Something like:

> This was a wonderful constraint, and it pushed me into work I would not otherwise have done. I especially loved seeing the community build alongside one another. Thank you for creating it. No response needed—I know you must be exhausted.

That deepens the actual friendship because it is private, personal, and asks for nothing.

### What risks the friendship

**Asking swyx for a call during contest week is the clearest unforced error.**

Even if Gene wants only a friendly conversation, swyx must consider:

- Whether other entrants received comparable access.
- Whether something he says could be interpreted as coaching.
- Whether he must disclose the interaction.
- Whether declining will feel awkward because Gene is a friend.
- Whether accepting could later taint Gene’s win.

A call transfers Gene’s ambiguity onto swyx. Don’t ask.

Private progress notes during the contest have a similar, smaller problem. Unless swyx requested them or an operational issue genuinely requires private handling, they resemble lobbying and consume scarce organizer attention.

Also harmful:

- Publishing the documentary before judging.
- Publicly emphasizing the longstanding friendship.
- Asking swyx to preview, bless, quote, repost, or react to the artifact.
- Tagging him repeatedly.
- Pre-announcing the donation.
- Producing praise-heavy content that makes him look obligated to acknowledge Gene.
- Calling the artifact a “gift” while expecting visibility or reciprocity.
- Using production polish to create a social campaign around the judging process.

The optic swyx needs is simple: **Gene contributed more than he extracted, received no privileged access, and would have behaved the same way if a stranger were judging.**
